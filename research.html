Research experience

What began as a fascination with language has grown into a question that continues to drive my research interests: how do people learn, and how can AI and technology scaffold and support learning? This fascination first led me to studying language acquisition in children with autism, and later towards analyzing learning gains with intelligent tutoring systems. Here is some of my previous research experience:

  • REU Intern @ Carnegie Mellon University Human-Computer Interaction Institute: Worked with Conrad Borchers and Vincent Aleven on Mathtutor, an AI-based tutoring system. Led analyses of learning gains, goal setting, and equity in a crossover study comparing tutor and paper practice. First author on the resulting paper, under review at LAK 2027.
  • PEAK Fellow & Research Assistant @ Northeastern University QLAB: Designed and conducted an independent research study investigating the reliability and language skill applicability of a novel phonemic statistical learning task. Coordinated recruitment, scheduling, and behavioral/ linguistic assessment sessions with children with and without autism.
  • Research Assistant @ Dialogue of Civilizations (España: Muchas y Diversas): Conducted three Spanish field linguistics research projects while studying in Granada, Barcelona, and San Sebastián.
  • Next: Coming soon..!

After I graduate, I plan to pursue a PhD in Human-Computer Interaction with a focus on learning sciences and technology. Ultimately, I hope to collaborate with industry partners to study learning technologies that will reach millions of students.
