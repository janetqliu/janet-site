Hi, I'm Janet Liu!

I'm an undergraduate at Northeastern University studying Data Science, Psychology, and Spanish. My research investigates people, learning, and technology. I study how students learn, how the tools they learn with shape that process, and whether those tools serve every learner equally well.

My path into research started at home. I grew up translating between my Mandarin-speaking grandparents and my English-speaking siblings, which made me curious about how people—especially younger students—learn. This curiosity led me to language acquisition research in children with autism at Northeastern's QLAB and later to Spanish linguistics research projects during a Dialogue of Civilizations program in Granada, Barcelona, and San Sebastián.

Since then, my questions have shifted toward learning technology. As an NSF REU Intern at Carnegie Mellon's Human-Computer Interaction Institute, I investigated how Mathtutor, an AI-based tutoring system, impacts middle school math learning gains.

Outside of research, I lead a product team at Code4Community, Northeastern's charitable software consultancy, and serve on the executive board of the Chinese Student Association. I also love to read, weightlift, and try as many restaurants and cafes as I can around Boston and beyond (check out my Beli: @jani4242)!
