Janet Liu

I'm an undergraduate at Northeastern University's Khoury College studying Data Science and Psychology. My research sits where people, learning, and technology meet: I study how students learn, how the tools they learn with shape that process, and whether those tools serve every learner equally well.

My path into research started at home. I grew up translating between my Mandarin-speaking grandparents and my English-speaking parents, which made me curious about how people pick up a language and what gets lost when they don't. That curiosity led me to language acquisition research at Northeastern's QLAB and to Spanish linguistics projects through Northeastern's Dialogue of Civilizations program.

Since then, my questions have shifted toward learning technology. As an HCI REU student at Carnegie Mellon's CATS Lab, I worked on Mathtutor, an AI-based tutoring system, analyzing whether digital tutoring narrows or widens the gap between students who start ahead and students who start behind. I'm first author on a paper from that work, currently under review at LAK 2027. This spring, I'll join the Brain Game Center as an AJC Merit Research Fellow to help build and validate digital math learning tools.

Outside the lab, I lead software teams for nonprofits at Code4Community and serve on the e-board of the Chinese Student Association. Use the buttons below to see my research and CV, or to get in touch.
